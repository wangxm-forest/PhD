\section{Chapter 1: Masting and reproductive traits}
\subsection{Research question}
Can reproductive traits provide evidence for the evolutionary mechanisms shaping masting patterns from the different hypotheses perspective?
\begin{enumerate}
\item Test whether traits associated with different evolutionary hypotheses are related to the strength of masting.
\end{enumerate}
\subsection{Methods and analysis}
\begin{enumerate}
\item We used phyloglm to analyse each trait separately.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Masting was treated as a binary response variable.
    \item Other traits included both continuous and categorical variables.
\end{enumerate}
\item We used NMDS to examine masting patterns using multiple reproductive traits.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Test whether combinations of traits associated with a particular hypothesis are more strongly related to masting patterns.
\end{enumerate}
\end{enumerate}
\subsection{Key results}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Seed weight and wind pollination were the only traits associated with strong masting.
\item These associations were detected only in angiosperms.
\item We found no evidence that all traits associated with a single hypothesis were consistently associated with masting.
\end{enumerate}
\subsection{Alternative explainations}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Masting is a population-level phenomenon
\item Different species selected under different hypotheses
\end{enumerate}
\subsection{Evolutionary perspective}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Potential selective pressures underlying the observed associations between reproductive traits and masting.
\item Possible explanations for the different patterns observed between angiosperms and gymnosperms.
\end{enumerate}
\subsection{Questions to discuss}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Alternative evolutionary perspectives for interpreting the relationship between seed weight and masting.
\item Possible explanations for the differences between angiosperms and gymnosperms.
\end{enumerate}
